# Guild Marketing OS Positioning Fit And Proof Method

Status: live private

Use when a Guild Marketing OS agent needs to turn approved context, customer research, market signal, or founder/project-leader input into positioning, fit boundaries, proof-backed claims, answer-ready language, pitch narrative, or campaign messages.

## Method

1. Identify the struggling moment: what changed, what is no longer working, and what is at stake.
2. Describe the desired progress the audience wants in the next quarter or two.
3. Map the honest candidate set: do nothing, keep it internal, use an agency or vendor, hire a specialist, change tools, or choose this project/company/offer.
4. Connect capability to relief: name the repeatable mechanism and the business change it enables.
5. Attach proof: approved source, customer evidence, case study, metric, project artifact, demo, public source, or `TBD`.
6. Address anxiety: fit, non-fit, implementation risk, switching cost, trust gap, and claim boundaries.
7. Name the metric hypothesis: what should improve if this positioning is right.

## Core Formula

Use capability, benefit, and proof instead of vague adjectives.

```text
Capability: This project/company can [repeatable mechanism].
Benefit: So [audience] can [desired progress] without [failure mode].
Proof: [approved evidence source or TBD].
```

Avoid unsupported language such as better, faster, strategic, proven, innovative, cutting-edge, world-class, transformational, or best-in-class unless the claim is attached to a concrete mechanism and approved evidence.

## Fit Forces

Every positioning packet should account for four forces:

- Push: what failure mode makes change necessary.
- Pull: what mechanism or outcome creates appeal.
- Habit: why the audience may keep the current approach.
- Anxiety: what risk, proof gap, or switching concern blocks action.

## Output Standard

Every positioning, fit, and proof packet should include:

- Struggling moment.
- Desired progress.
- Candidate set, including doing nothing.
- Capability, benefit, and proof map.
- Fit and non-fit boundaries.
- Objections and anxiety reducers.
- Proof anchors and missing evidence.
- Claim table with status: approved, user-supplied, source claim, inference, assumption, missing, or do-not-use.
- Metric hypothesis and dashboard signal.
- Recommended surface: context artifact, website block, answer-ready block, pitch section, campaign angle, sales asset, or `TBD`.

## Handoff Targets

- Company Context Builder receives fit boundaries, proof map, claim constraints, and workspace-context summary inputs.
- ICP receives struggling moments, alternatives, anxieties, fit, and non-fit.
- Messaging receives capability, benefit, proof, objections, and answer-ready blocks.
- Branding And Pitch Deck receives pitch narrative, market-shift framing, and proof hierarchy.
- Answer Engine And Web Readiness receives direct-answer blocks, claim tables, query gaps, and proof surfaces.
- Campaigns And Paid Media receives campaign angles, audience-message fit, and claim boundaries.

## Claim Rules

Do not turn positioning into public copy until the claim table is reviewable. If proof is missing, use hypothesis language or route the claim to an approval gate.
